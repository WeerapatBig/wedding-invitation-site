# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Primary audience**: wedding guests of Waranphat (Ice) & Rakpon (Tao) — a mixed-generation Thai guest list spanning parents, aunts, uncles, grandparents, cousins, and friends, plus international guests. Most receive the invitation as a link shared via LINE or messaging apps and open it on their phone.

**Context of use**: recipients open the card once or twice — to learn the schedule, save the date, find the venue, screenshot details, and forward to family group chats. Older relatives may show it to others on their phone in person. A few will revisit it the morning of the wedding to re-check the timing.

**Job to be done**: communicate the ceremony schedule and venue with zero friction, while making the recipient *feel* the warmth, romance, and significance of the day — not just read about it. The card is both a notice and a keepsake.

## Product Purpose

A bilingual (Thai + English) digital wedding invitation for **Ice & Tao**, 27 June 2026, at a beach venue in Thailand.

**Ceremony timeline**
- 07:09 — พิธีสงฆ์ (Buddhist monk ceremony)
- 09:09 — พิธีแห่ขันหมาก (Khan Maak procession)
- 10:09 — ยกน้ำชา (Tea ceremony)
- 12:00 — รับประทานอาหาร (Reception lunch)

**Venue**: ศาลาประชาคมอ่าวอุดม (Ao Udom Community Hall) — Google Maps: `https://www.google.com/maps?ftid=0x3102b9dc66aab801:0x324502b197b41df8`. Geographically by Ao Udom Bay; the "by the sea" feeling in copy is mood-of-place, not the literal venue.

**Ambient audio (opt-in only)**: New West — *Those Eyes*. Used as a quiet underlayer if the guest chooses to enable it. Never autoplays.

**Success criteria**
- A guest can find the date, time, and venue within five seconds of opening the page.
- The first impression feels cinematic — golden hour, ocean, intimacy — not templated.
- Older relatives can read every word on a phone without zooming.
- The bilingual presentation feels intentional and equal, never bolted-on.

## Brand Personality

**Three words**: cinematic, warm, intimate.

**Voice**: tender and quiet. The card speaks softly; it doesn't perform. Bilingual copy is sincere in both languages — Thai is never a translation afterthought.

**Emotional goal**: when the recipient closes the card, they should feel they've just watched the opening minute of a film about two people in love — not received a notification.

**Atmosphere**: golden hour at the beach. Warm sun on skin, slow light, ocean horizon, sand. Soft amber, terracotta, rose, dune. Photographic rather than illustrative.

## Anti-references

- **Stock wedding-template sites**: cursive script headlines, gold-foil flourishes, generic floral SVGs, "save the date" in spinning hearts. Every visual that signals "wedding website builder" is out.
- **Glittery gold**: no metallic gradients, no glossy gold foil, no animated sparkles. Warmth comes from photography and light — never from decorative shine.
- **Pastel + copperplate-script clichés**: blush watercolor washes with calligraphic display type. The most saturated wedding look on the internet; avoid by default.
- **Identical card grids and oversized monogram-in-a-circle**: standard wedding-builder layouts that signal "template."
- **SaaS / tech-startup feel**: clean white background, rounded buttons, neutral sans on a flat canvas. This is not a product landing page.

## Design Principles

1. **Let the photograph carry the warmth.** Cinematic feeling comes from a strong golden-hour photograph, generous breathing room, and slow type — not from decorative motifs piled on top.
2. **Thai is a first-class citizen.** Thai script is never a smaller subtitle of English. Pair a quality Thai display face with its Western counterpart; tune line-height and tracking for Thai's tone marks; never let English typography dictate the layout at Thai's expense.
3. **Quiet on the phone, expansive in the moment.** Default to clear, calm, readable on a 360-wide phone for older relatives. Reserve atmosphere — slow fades, warm overlays, ambient audio — for the hero, not every scroll.
4. **Specific, not generic.** The card belongs to Ice & Tao, not to "a couple." Real names, real ceremony schedule with Thai terminology preserved, real venue. No placeholder language anywhere.
5. **Cinematic, not theatrical.** Restraint over performance. One held shot beats five clever transitions. If a motion or effect doesn't deepen the feeling, it leaves.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG AA contrast** across the warm palette: body text on golden / terracotta backgrounds must be checked against its actual rendered background, not the intended one. Warm-on-warm fails easily.
- **Body type ≥ 16px on phone**, 17–18px for the older-relative tier. Thai script typically needs ~1.05× the Western type size to feel comparable in weight.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`**: replace fades, parallax, and slow reveals with instant transitions. The card must still feel composed without motion.
- **Audio**: never autoplays. Provide a visible mute / unmute toggle. Honor system mute.
- **Single-handed phone use**: primary actions (RSVP if added, view map, save date) reachable in the bottom third of the viewport.
- **Bilingual layout integrity**: Thai and English must both be readable side-by-side or via a clear, persistent toggle. Neither language should ever overlap, clip, or render below the other in a way that implies hierarchy.
